EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD): What Apparel Importers Need to Know for 2026/2027

Share Briefing:

The European Union’s Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) represents the most profound regulatory shift for apparel manufacturing and international fashion brands in thirty years. Unlike voluntary corporate codes of conduct, the CSDDD imposes legally enforceable civil liability and substantial revenue-based penalties on brands that fail to identify, prevent, and remediate human rights and environmental violations within their global upstream supply chains.

1. Scope & Enforcement Timeline

The directive applies directly to EU-based companies with over 1,000 employees and net turnover exceeding €450 million worldwide, as well as non-EU companies generating equivalent turnover within the EU single market. Crucially, the due diligence requirements cascade down into every direct (Tier 1) and indirect (Tier 2/3) manufacturing partner operating outside Europe.

Key Enforcement Threshold: Member states are empowered to levy fines up to 5% of the company’s worldwide net turnover and hold executive officers personally liable for systemic failure to prevent supply chain abuses.

2. Key Human Rights Mandates

Apparel importers must demonstrate documented oversight over three non-negotiable labor baselines:

  • Fair Living Wage Guarantees: Proving that factory wage structures meet independently calculated living wage benchmarks rather than merely minimum local statutory rates.
  • Working Hour Caps: Enforcing strict limits on voluntary overtime, ensuring 48-hour maximum standard workweeks and at least one consecutive 24-hour rest period per seven days.
  • Workplace Safety & Medical Infrastructure: Certified fire escape access, on-site registered nurses, free female sanitation facilities, and functional worker grievance committees.

3. Environmental & Water Due Diligence

Textile dyeing and wet processing represent the highest regulatory risk under CSDDD environmental provisions. Brands sourcing dyed knits and wovens must prove that finishing mills operate fully compliant wastewater treatment infrastructure.

Figure 1.1: In-house spectrophotometer and water chemical oxygen demand (COD) testing chamber. ZDHC Level 3 Verified
"By 2027, failure to verify Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) effluent compliance at your dye supplier will be treated with identical legal severity as shipping hazardous chemicals across borders."

4. Chain of Custody & Transaction Certificates (TCs)

Under the EU directive and parallel US UFLPA enforcement, paper-based declarations are no longer sufficient. Sourcing desks must demand digital batch tracking connecting raw organic cotton bales or recycled fiber pellet lots directly to each export carton's bill of lading.
